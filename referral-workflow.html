01 / The challenge

A process that
lost its momentum.

Referrals moved between staff through physical handoffs. When information was misplaced or delayed, employees spent time locating referrals and following up instead of moving requests forward.

The challenge wasn't simply to move paperwork online. It was to create a more reliable way to know who owned the next step and ensure referrals kept moving.

02 / Discovery

Listen first.
Design second.

I gathered feedback through staff surveys and researched potential approaches. I used those findings to identify recurring pain points, bring together ideas from the people involved, and shape a solution around the district's existing systems.

This research-led approach helped ensure the redesigned process addressed the day-to-day realities of the people using it.

A better workflow should reduce the effort required to keep work moving.

03 / Solution

Connected by
conditions.

I independently designed, configured, and implemented a workflow connecting SWIS with the school's email system. Referrals were digitally assigned to responsible individuals, and completing a step or signing off triggered an automatic email notification to the next person in the process.

By replacing manual follow-up with condition-based notifications, the workflow made handoffs more consistent and helped establish clearer responsibility at each stage.
